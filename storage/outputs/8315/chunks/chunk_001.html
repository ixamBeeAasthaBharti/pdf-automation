1. Low Per Capita Income
Per Capita Income in Rural and Urban Areas
•
Government compiles estimates of rural and urban income, in terms of Per Capita Net
Value Added (NVA), only in the base year of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
series, which is currently 2011-12. The per capita NVA for rural and urban area
was Rs.40,925 and Rs.98,435 respectively in the year 2011-12.
•
All India annual per capita Net National Income (NNI) for 2014-15 and 2022-23 at
current prices are Rs. 86,647 and Rs. 1,72,000 respectively illustrating
achievement in this regard.

Excessive dependence of agriculture and primary producing
•
Indian economy can be best described by too much reliance on agriculture making it
the primary producing and 72.4 per cent of workforce still resides in rural areas .
•
But though times are changing, there's still a long way to go. As per numerous studies
conducted recently, it is now apparent that still about two third of rural income is
generated from non-agricultural activities.
•
Likewise, it’s a fact that more than half of the value supplemented in manufacturing
sector in India is contributed by rural areas.

3. Existence of chronic unemployment and under-employment
•
The degree to which a society can provide employment to the employment seekers is
one of the most crucial indicators of the well-being of any society. Therefore, the
unemployment rate, which measures the incapability to provide such employment
becomes the most significant indicator to measure the health of any modern economy.
•
Rapid growth of population coupled with incompetent growth of secondary and tertiary
occupations are considered responsible for the occurrence of chronic unemployment
and under-employment problem in our country.
As per the Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy (March 2023)
•
Unemployment rate in India increased to 7.45 per cent in February 2023 from 7.14
per cent in January 2023 . The number of unemployed persons in the labor force in
India increased from 31.5 million to 33 million .
•
Unemployment rate in rural India climbed from 6.48 per cent in January to 7.23
per cent in February, which is a 0.75 percentage points increase.
•
The number of unemployed persons in rural India increased by around 2.3 million .
•
This rise in unemployment in February comes as a surprise because rural
unemployment was on a downward trajectory in the preceding four months. Between
January and February 2023 , the rural labor participation rate remained almost
unchanged at around 40.9 per cent.
•
There was, however, a marginal increase in the size of the labor force. The labor force
in rural India expanded from 299.8 million in January to 300 million in February
2023 .
4. Poor rate of capital formation
•
Gross Capital Formation (GCF) at the current as well as the constant prices is
estimated by two approaches: –
(i) Through flow of funds , derived as Gross Saving plus net capital flow from
Rest of the World (RoW); and
(ii) By the commodity flow approach , derived by the type of assets.
•
The estimates of GCF through the flow of funds approach are treated as the firmer
estimates. GCF by industry of use and by institutional sectors does not include
‘valuables’ and th erefore, these estimates are lower than the estimates available from
commodity flow approach.
•
Poor rate of capital formation in rural India is mainly because of three reasons:

5. Inequality in the distribution of wealth
•
Acc to World inequality report 2022, The average national income of the Indian adult
population is €PPP7,400 (or INR204,200).* While the bottom 50% earns €PPP2 000
(INR53,610), the top 10% earns more than 20 times more (€PPP42 500 or
INR1,166,520). While the top 10% and top 1% hold respectively 57% and 22% of
total national income, the bottom 50% share has gone down to 13% .
•
India stands out as a poor and very unequal country, with an affluent elite and Extreme
income inequalities in India
•
Gender inequalities in India are very high . The female labor income share is equal
to 18%. This is significantly lower than the average in Asia (21%, excluding China).
This value is one of the lowest in the world, slightly higher than the average share in
Middle East (15%). The significant increase observed since 1990 (+8 p.p.) has been
insufficient to lift women’s labor income share to the regional average.
•
As per the Socio-Economic and Caste Census (SECC) survey, a staggering 92% of
rural households reported their maximum income below Rs 10,000 per month .
•
SECC reports that only about 10% of rural households have salaried jobs. Of these,
about two thirds are in public sector, and a third, 6.4 lakh in all, work regular private
sector jobs.
•
Moreover, only less than 5% of the rural households pay income tax or professional
tax. This is mainly because agricultural income is not taxed and with over 92%
households earning less than Rs.10,000, most don't qualify.
6. Low level of technology
•
The rural economy of our country is suffering from technological backwardness.
Obsolescent production techniques are largely being used in both the agricultural and
industrial sectors in India.
•
Employment growth is grave in low-productivity agriculture that accounts for
nearly three-fourth of the poor population. At the same time, automation impedes
to create more unemployment. Moreover, it would be very highly difficult to adopt
modern technology in Indian productive system as most of its labor is either untrained
or illiterate or unskilled or all of them.
•
Currently, GOI is working to transform the country's rural economy and create skillful
jobs in rural areas through its Digital India programme.
•
For the assessed 156 million rural households in India , most living in poverty as
per the India’s National Sample Survey Organization (NSSO), there is a dire need for
investment in transportation, power and internet literacy to create more employment
for youth and women in rural areas.

8. Lack of infrastructure
•
Lack of infrastructural facilities is one of the impending problems from which the
economy of India has been suffering till date. Though a number of initiatives have been
taken to tackle this issue, there is still long way to go.
•
These infrastructural facilities include Communication and transportation facilities,
banking and credit facilities, electricity generation and distribution, health and
educational institutes, economic organization etc.
•
The two most important sectors of rural India, i. e., agriculture and industry could
not make much progress in the absence of the required infrastructural facilities
in the country.
•
Moreover, due to the absence of proper and required infrastructural facilities,
development potential of various regions of the country largely remained under-
utilized.
•

9. Poor quality of human capital
•
Indian economy to a great extent is suffering from its poor quality of human capital.
•
Mass illiteracy is the root cause of this problem and at the same time illiteracy is
retarding the process of economic growth of our country.
•
The results of 2011 census reveal that there has been an increase in literacy in the
country. The literacy rate in the country is 74.04 per cent, 82.14 for males and
65.46 for females.
•
Kerala retained its position by being on top with a 93.91 per cent literacy rate ,
closely followed by Lakshadweep (92.28 per cent) and Mizoram (91.58 per cent).
•
Bihar with a literacy rate of 63.82 per cent ranks last in the country preceded by
Arunachal Pradesh (66.95 per cent) and Rajasthan (67.06 per cent).

10. Demographic characteristics
• The demographic characteristics of India are not at all satisfactory as these are

• A high dependency ratio can cause serious problems for a country if a large proportion

11. Inadequate development of economic organization
• Poor economic organization is yet another important characteristic of the Rural
Indian economy. To attain economic development at an adequate rate, certain
institutions are very much essential.
• As for example, for mobilization of savings and to meet other financial needs, more
particularly in the rural areas, development of certain financial institutions is very
much needed.
• In India the availability or expansion of financial institutions is still inadequate in the
rural areas.
• There is the pressing need to develop certain credit agencies for advancing loan to
small farmers on easy terms as well as to provide long term and medium-term loan
to rural industries.
• Moreover, all these require maintenance of honest and efficient administrative
machinery which there is dearth in India.

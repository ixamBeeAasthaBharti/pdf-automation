RBI unveils major push to strengthen Co-op Banks: MoS

RBI Reforms for Cooperative Banks (2026)

Overall Objective
  • RBI, in consultation with the Government of India, introduced reforms to strengthen the cooperative banking ecosystem.
  • Focus areas:
    • Financial health
    • Governance
    • Credit flow
    • Digital inclusion
  • Information shared by MoS Finance Pankaj Chaudhary in Rajya Sabha.
Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Boost
  • Loans given by banks to National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC) for on-lending to cooperatives now classified as Priority Sector Lending.
  • Effective date: January 19, 2026.
  • Applies to banks excluding:
    • RRBs
    • Urban Cooperative Banks
    • Small Finance Banks
    • Local Area Banks
  • Expected to:
    • Increase credit availability
    • Support cooperative societies & rural institutions
Strengthening Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs)
  • New branch openings allowed for UCBs.
  • Housing loan limit increased:
    • From 10% → 25% of total loans & advances.
  • Aim: Expand lending capacity and urban outreach.
Governance Reforms
  • Amendments to the Banking Regulation Act.
  • Tenure of cooperative bank directors extended:
    • From 8 years → 10 years.
  • Intended to improve stability and management continuity.
Digital Inclusion Initiatives
  • AePS onboarding costs reduced → encourages digital transactions.
  • Creation of new shared technology support institutions:
    • NUCFDC: Umbrella IT & operational support body for UCBs.
    • Sahakar Sarthi: Shared Services Entity for Rural Cooperative Banks.
Depositor Protection
  • All cooperative banks continue under DICGC insurance cover.
  • Protection up to ₹5 lakh per depositor.
  • Helps build trust & depositor confidence.

Retail inflation at 2.75% in new Consumer Price Index with 2024 as the base year

Latest Inflation Data

  • MoSPI released a new CPI series with 2024 as base year (earlier: 2012).
  • Retail inflation (CPI) for January 2026 stood at 2.75%.
  • First release under the new base year → no direct historical comparison yet.

Expansion in Coverage

  • Total items covered: increased to 358 (earlier 299).
    • Goods: 308 (earlier 259)
    • Services: 50 (earlier 40)
  • Reflects updated consumption patterns from HCES 2023–24.
  • Shows structural change in economy, consumption behaviour, and household spending.

Wider Data Collection Network

  • Rural markets covered: 1,465 (earlier 1,181)
  • Urban markets covered: 1,395 (earlier 1,114)
  • New addition: data from 12 online marketplaces
  • Ensures more representative and granular inflation measurement.

More Detailed Classification

  • Groups expanded from 6 to 12 broad categories.
  • Provides granular data for better policy analysis.
  • Weights revised based on HCES 2023–24 consumption trends.

Major Changes in Weightage

Food & Beverages
  • Weight reduced to 36.75% (earlier 45.86%).
  • Lower food weight may make headline inflation less volatile.
Housing & Utilities
  • Expanded to include water, electricity, gas, fuels.
  • Combined weight increased to 17.67% (earlier 10.07% for housing alone).
Paan, Tobacco & Intoxicants
  • Weight increased to 2.99% (earlier 2.38%).
Clothing & Footwear
  • Weight reduced to 2.38% (earlier 6.53%).

Newly Emphasised Categories (with Weights)

  • Furnishings & household maintenance: 4.47%
  • Health: 6.1%
  • Transport: 8.8%
  • Information & communication: 3.61%
  • Recreation, sports & culture: 1.52%
  • Education services: 3.33%
  • Restaurants & accommodation: 3.35%
  • Personal care & miscellaneous: 5.04%

Policy Significance

  • Basket aligned with latest expenditure patterns → more realistic inflation signals.
  • Helps in better calibration of monetary and fiscal policy.
  • Reflects changing structure of Indian economy over the last decade.

Historical Data Adjustment

  • Index values available from January 2025 under new method.
  • Linking factor provided to estimate CPI back to 2013.
  • However, year-on-year inflation comparison currently possible only for January 2026.

India–US trade deal seen lifting surplus by $45 bn, exports could top $100 bn annually: Report

Overall Economic Impact

  • India–US trade relationship set for a major reset after new bilateral trade agreement.
  • India’s annual trade surplus with the US projected to rise by $45 billion.
  • Export potential could exceed $100 billion per year.
  • Net impact estimated to:
    • Add ~1.1% to India’s GDP
    • Save about $3 billion annually in foreign exchange due to lower import duties.
  • Total trade surplus with the US may cross $90 billion annually.

Tariff Advantage & Competitiveness

  • Reciprocal tariff on Indian goods reduced to 18%.
  • Makes India more competitive than key Asian exporters like:
    • Vietnam
    • ASEAN countries
  • Improves price competitiveness of Indian exports in the US market.

Export Growth Potential

  • India’s goods exports to the US may rise by ~$100 billion annually.
  • Driven by:
    • Tariff reductions
    • Large unmet demand in the US market.
  • US imports exceed $3 trillion, while India’s share is only ~3% → huge growth scope.

Top Export Sectors Likely to Benefit

  • Electrical machinery
  • Engineering goods
  • Pharmaceuticals
  • Vehicles
  • Gems & jewellery
  • Textiles
  • Chemicals
  • Seafood
  • Top 15 categories alone could add ~$97 billion in exports.

Agriculture Sector Gains

  • 75% of India’s agri exports to the US to face zero additional tariffs.
  • Key products benefiting:
    • Rice
    • Spices
    • Tea & coffee
    • Oilseeds & nuts
    • Seafood
  • India already has an agriculture trade surplus of ~$1.3 billion with the US.
  • US imports:
    • ~25% of rice from India.
  • Expected boost to:
    • Farmers
    • Fisheries
    • Plantation sectors

Import Commitments by India

  • India to reduce/eliminate tariffs on many US industrial & agricultural goods.
  • Intention to import $500 billion worth of US products over 5 years, including:
    • Energy
    • Aircraft
    • Technology products
    • Precious metals
  • Imports from US may rise by ~$55 billion annually.
  • Despite this, trade balance remains in India’s favour.

China+1 Supply Chain Opportunity

  • Agreement supports China+1 diversification strategy.
  • Encourages US companies to invest in India for:
    • Value addition
    • Using India as a global export base
  • Particularly strong opportunity in:
    • Electronics
    • Electrical equipment

Strategic & Economic Significance

  • Strengthens India’s role in global supply chains.
  • Likely to:
    • Boost manufacturing growth
    • Increase exports
    • Improve external balances
  • Marks a major shift in India–US economic engagement.

Chidambaranar Port Authority, Tuticorin Secures Platinum Rating for sustainable, eco-friendly buildings & Energy Efficiency Certification for energy-efficient, sustainable buildings

Major Achievement

  • V.O. Chidambaranar Port Authority becomes India’s first Major Port to receive IGBC Platinum Rating (highest certification by Indian Green Building Council).
  • Recognition for sustainable infrastructure, energy efficiency, and green practices.

Energy & Sustainability Features

  • Administration Building operates on 100% renewable energy:
    • 89% electricity from on-site solar panels
    • 11% from off-site renewable sources
  • Equipped with:
    • Rooftop solar panels
    • High Solar Reflectance Index (SRI) roof coating → reduces Urban Heat Island effect
  • Supports energy conservation & decarbonisation goals.

Water & Resource Efficiency

  • Use of:
    • IoT-enabled water meters
    • Rainwater harvesting systems
    • Water-saving plumbing fixtures
    • Indoor Air Quality (IAQ) monitoring
  • Achieved 37% reduction in potable water usage.
  • 100% wastewater treated & reused through on-site Sewage Treatment Plant (STP).

Green Campus Initiatives

  • Campus has 70%+ green cover through extensive tree plantation.
  • Seven IGBC Accredited Professionals oversee sustainability implementation.

Health, Safety & Inclusivity Measures

  • No Smoking Policy in the building.
  • Use of eco-friendly cleaning chemicals.
  • Accessibility features include:
    • Non-slippery ramps
    • Differently-abled-friendly toilets
    • Electric wheelchairs
    • Lifts with braille & audio assistance

BEE ‘Shunya’ & ‘Shunya Plus’ Certifications

  • Awarded by Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), Ministry of Power.
Shunya Certification
  • Indicates Net Zero Energy Building.
  • Building produces as much energy as it consumes annually.
  • Energy Performance Index (EPI): 0–10 kWh/m²/year.
  • Awarded to:
    • Administrative Building
    • Guest House
Shunya Plus Certification
  • Indicates Net Positive Energy Building.
  • Produces more energy than it consumes.
  • EPI: Less than 0 kWh/m²/year.
  • Awarded to:
    • Port Hospital
    • CISF Barracks

Policy Alignment

  • Initiatives aligned with:
    • Maritime India Vision 2030
    • Harit Sagar – Green Port Guidelines
  • Supports sustainable port infrastructure & inclusive growth.

India as Chair of the Kimberley Process 2026

Why in News?

  • India assumed Chairmanship of the Kimberley Process (KP) for 2026.
  • Positions India at the centre of global diamond governance.
  • Enhances India’s role as a Global South voice in shaping reforms in the diamond value chain.

What is the Kimberley Process (KP)?

  • Initiated in 2000 by Southern African countries.
  • Objective: Regulate trade in rough diamonds and prevent conflict diamonds.
  • Conflict diamonds: Used by rebel groups to finance wars against legitimate governments.
  • Not a formal international organisation or legally binding treaty.
  • Operates through national laws, with support from:
    • Governments
    • Industry
    • Civil society

Participation

  • 60 participants representing 86 countries.
  • Covers 99.8% of global rough diamond production.

Kimberley Process Certification Scheme (KPCS)

  • Launched in 2003.
  • Requires:
    • Every shipment of rough diamonds to carry a tamper-resistant KP certificate.
    • Trade only between certified member countries.
    • Sharing of accurate production & trade data for transparency.
  • India is a founding member.
  • Enforcement done by individual member countries.

India’s Diamond Industry

Diamond Resources

  • Mining dates back to 5th century BCE.
  • Historic hubs:
    • Golconda (Telangana) – global trading centre in 16th–17th century.
  • Early sources:
    • Krishna, Mahanadi, Panna belts.
  • Majhgawan Mine (Panna, MP):
    • India’s only operating commercial diamond mine.
    • Panna diamonds have GI Tag.

Global Market Dominance

  • India is a major importer of rough diamonds (from UAE, Belgium, Russia).
  • ~90% of world’s diamonds processed in India.
  • Accounts for ~75% of global diamond trade turnover (by value).
  • Major centres:
    • Surat
    • Mumbai

Lab-Grown Diamonds (LGDs)

  • India produced 3 million+ LGDs (2023) → 15% of global output.
  • Manufactured using:
    • CVD (Chemical Vapour Deposition)
    • HPHT (High Pressure High Temperature)

Government Support

  • ₹242 crore grant (Union Budget FY24) for LGD research centre at IIT Madras.
  • Customs duty removed on imported carbon seeds (Budget FY26).
  • 100% FDI allowed in LGD sector.
  • Aim: Make India a global manufacturing hub, not just a polishing centre.

India’s Strategic Role

  • Major exporter of:
    • Polished diamonds
    • Lab-grown diamonds
  • Key markets:
    • USA
    • China
    • UAE
    • Hong Kong
  • Strong position in global value chain → influence global governance norms.

Challenges in Kimberley Process

  • Narrow definition of conflict diamonds (only rebel-linked violence).
  • “Mixed origin” loophole allows re-entry of conflict diamonds.
  • Consensus-based veto power slows reforms.
  • No permanent secretariat weakens monitoring.
  • Applies only to rough diamonds, not polished ones.
  • Rising popularity of LGDs may reduce demand for natural diamonds.

Reforms India Can Push as Chair

  • Expand definition of conflict diamonds (include human rights concerns).
  • Promote blockchain-based certification for traceability.
  • Encourage independent audits & transparency.
  • Support African producer nations through:
    • Technical hubs
    • Certification support
  • Ensure diamond revenues benefit local mining communities.
  • Align KP with Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

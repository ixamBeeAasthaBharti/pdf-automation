India is primarily a rural country with almost 65 per cent of
country's population and 56.9 per cent of labor force
inhabits in rural areas (according to 2011 census ( Periodic
Labour Force Survey May 2025)
•
Rural economy comprises 46 per cent of national income. Regardless of the rise in
urbanization, it is projected that more than half of India's population would still be rural
by 2050. Consequently, rural economy’s growth and development and its population
form key to overall growth and inclusive development of our country.
•
Contrary to the common perception about the preponderance of agriculture in rural
economy, it’s been found that, about two third of rural income is now generated
from non-agricultural activities.
•
Since ages, the Indian village has been a self-contained and self-sufficient economy.
Rural Economy in India: Introduction
•
Post-Independence period witnessed reconstruction and development of rural India as
a pivotal role in economic planning, which has caused a rapid transformation in the
structure of Indian rural economy.
•
Several various welfare services were initiated, such as launching Primary healthcare
centers, opening up of schools, improving the means of transport and communication,
and spread of mass media services to rural areas, etc. These welfare services have
significantly affected the rural life.
Features of Rural Economy
Following are few of the distinguished features of the present-day rural economy in India
1. Commercialization of Agriculture:
•
Now a days, a large part of rural economy has been opened up, which has made
commercialization of agriculture possible.

•
The key factors that contributed to the commercialization of agriculture are:
o Huge production and good productivity gains have steered agriculture to be a
profitable proposition.
o Advanced technology in agricultural operations has increased the production
to a great extent.
o Vast expansion of road transport reduced the distance between Urban and
Rural areas.
o Development of cooperative marketing structure coupled with regulated
markets has helped the farmers get respite from village system of
adthiyas/middlemen and private moneylenders.
2. Urbanism and its impact on Rural society:
•
The past seven decades, post-independence to India, especially in the 21st century,
India has witnessed the rural society, getting exposed to the waves of urbanism.
•
Rural society has imbibed most of the features of urban areas which have been
modified and diluted, which led to a new trend of urbanism called rural-urbanism.
•
Rural urbanism has created new social institutions, that were absent in the customary
rural set up, which led to modernization of rural society.
Two reasons can be accredited for such a situation.
I.
Interaction of the rural folk with their urban counterparts has created awareness about
consumer goods among them, coupled with green revolution, wherein the income
levels of the rural people have increased, which made such consumption of goods
possible.
II.
The introduction of modern agricultural technology has resulted in the green revolution,
which supplied the industrial goods such as fertilizers, machinery, etc.
•
Current day, rural India is seen as the potential market to the industrial goods. Rural
areas have been seeing many industries flourishing in their areas, which are
considered to be rich in the resources needed for the setting up of such industries.
3. Institutional Participation:
•
Post-independence, the government had taken the charge of rural reconstruction and
development. It had also brought about important changes in the relations of
production.
•
Abolition of middlemen/intermediaries, tenancy security, ceiling on land holdings, etc.,
were some of the important legislations made, that improved the relations of
production.
•
Numerous rural development programmes have been taken up. These programmes
contributed tremendously in bringing about modernization in villages and village folk.

Structure of the Indian Rural Economy
•
India is predominantly an agrarian society with t wo third of its population living in
rural areas. Rural economy has conventionally been associated with agriculture.
•
Agriculture and related activities are the main source of livelihood for the rural
folk. Hence, the performance of agriculture sector, plays a critical role in the economic
growth of our country.
•
Allied activities of agriculture include livestock that provide milk and by-
products of milk, meat and by-products of meat, forming a major supplier of food, raw
materials, semi-finished and finished products.
•
Another significant nature-based activity that largely forms a distinct sector ,
associated with agriculture, is Forests. Its key products include industrial and fuel wood
of different types used for numerous purposes and in innumerable ways.
•
Apart from the above, there are minor forest produce viz, Canes and Bamboos, Bidi
leaves, Honey, Lac etc.
•
Besides this, another sector of agriculture includes - fishing, comprising of both
Marine fish and Inland/Freshwater fish.
•
The rural economy is also dependent on village/rural industries. These are mostly
traditional industries and are conventionally artisan based. The products
include Handicrafts, Khadi, Leather and many more.
•
The indirect businesses/industries thriving entirely on agriculture and allied activities
are Fertilizer industry, pesticide industry, seed industry, tractor/field equipment
manufacturing industry.
•
Rural India’s non -farm sector is estimated to be over Rs. 45,000 crores, including
the Field equipment/tractors business. India's low consumption of fertilizers, and
pesticides, high-yielding varieties, when compared to other countries indicates a huge
potential for creating employment and increase income of the rural folk in the rural
areas.
•
Village and small industries (VSI) sector consisting of two broad sub-sectors,
viz, modern small industries and traditional industries , is instituted by eight specific
groups’ viz. Handicrafts, Handloom, Sericulture, Coir, Village Industries, Khadi, Power
looms and Small-Scale Industries. The last two items represent the modern group of
industries, the others being traditional industries.

Women Empowerment
•
The transformative potential of Self-Help Groups (SHGs), exemplified through their key
role in the on-ground response to Covid-19, has served as the fulcrum of rural
development through women empowerment. India has around 1.2 crore SHGs, 88 per
cent being all-women SHGs.
•
The SHG Bank Linkage Project (SHG-BLP), launched in 1992 , has blossomed into
the world’s largest microfinance project. The SHG -BLP covers 14.2 crore families
through 119 lakh SHGs with savings deposits of Rs. 47,240.5 crore and 67 lakh groups
with collateral-free loans outstanding of Rs. 1,51,051.3 crore, as on 31 March 2022.
•
The number of SHGs credit linked has grown at a CAGR of 10.8 per cent during the
last ten years (FY13 to FY22). Notably, SHGs’ bank repayment is more than 96 per
cent, underscoring their credit discipline and reliability.
•
The share of women among those trained under PMKVY has Increased from 42.7 per
cent in 2015-16 to 58.0 per cent 2024-25.
•
A total of 3997 Gender Resource Centres(GRCs) are operating across 18 States and
Uts under DAY-NRLM, empowering women and gender-diverse persons to address
Violence and access their rights.
•
In rural areas, female agricultural employment rose from 73.2 per cent in 2017-18 to
76.9 in 2023-24 per cent, while male participation decreased from 55.0 per cent To
49.4 per cent, reflecting a shift towards non-agricultural sectors during the same Period

Water and Sanitation
•
On the 73rd Independence Day, 15 August 2019, the Jal Jeevan Mission
(JJM) was announced, to be implemented in partnership with States, to provide , tap
water connection to every rural household and public institutions in villages like
schools, Anganwadi centres, ashram shalas (tribal residential schools), health centres
etc.
•
Since the launch of the Mission, 12.2 crore households provided with tap water
connections under Jal Jeevan Mission (as of 27 January 2025)
•
There are 2,160 water quality laboratories, 1,570 of which are accredited by National
Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories, ensuring the safety of
drinking water.
• Mission Amrit Sarovar aimed at developing and rejuvenating of 75 water bodies in
each district of the country during the Amrit Varsh - 75th year of independence.
•
The mission was launched by the Government on National Panchayati Raj Day in
2022. Against an initial target of 50,000 Amrit Sarovars, a total of 93,291 Amrit Sarovar
sites were identified, works commenced on more than 54,047 sites and out of these
sites were works began, a total of 68,843 Amrit Sarovars (ponds) constructed under
Mission Amrit Sarovarl have been constructed.
• Swachh Bharat Mission Grameen (SBM-G) achieved open defecation-free
(ODF) status in the first phase. Phase II of SBM-G is implemented from 2020-21 to
2024-25, with a focus on converting the villages from ODF to ODF Plus.
•
An ODF plus Progress is captured in three categories namely aspiring, rising and
model. The goal Of SBM-G Phase- II is to make all the villages ODF Plus, i.e. “Model
Category”. An ODF Plus village in the model category is one that is sustaining its ODF
status, plus having Arrangements for both solid and liquid waste management,
observing visual cleanliness (minimal litter, minimal stagnant wastewater and no
plastic waste dump in public Places) and displaying ODF plus information, education
and communication messages.
•
During April to November 2024, 1.92 lakh villages were incrementally declared ODF
Plus under the model category, taking the total number of ODF Plus villages to 3.64
Lakh.

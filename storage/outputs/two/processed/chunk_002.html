Indian Semiconductor Startups Raise $206 Million Since 2022

  • Homegrown semiconductor startups have raised around $206 million across 51 funding rounds since 2022, with investment accelerating in 2026 as investors increasingly back more mature chip ventures.

Funding Trends

  • Semiconductor startups raised $61.9 million in H1 2026, equivalent to 81% of the $76.6 million raised during the whole of 2025.
  • Funding rounds declined even as capital deployed increased:
    • 2024: 16 rounds
    • 2025: 13 rounds
    • H1 2026: 7 rounds
  • The trend indicates greater investor concentration on startups that have progressed further in product development and commercialisation.

Government-Private Capital Link

  • Of the 24 chip-design projects supported under the Design-Linked Incentive (DLI) programme, 14 subsequently raised institutional venture capital.
  • These 14 companies collectively raised $100.8 million across their first and second funding rounds.
  • Six companies have already completed follow-on funding rounds worth a combined $53.6 million.
  • C2i Semiconductors, NetraSemi, Morphing Machines and Mindgrove Technologies together account for approximately 56% of private capital raised by DLI-backed companies.

Sector Maturity

  • Several semiconductor startups are progressing rapidly from seed funding to Series A rounds, with some doing so within 7–22 months.
  • Seven recent Series A rounds highlighted in the report collectively raised $73.7 million.
  • This amount represents around one-third of all semiconductor startup capital raised in India since 2022.
  • The trend indicates growing investor confidence in startups that have moved beyond early-stage technology development.

Strategic Investment

  • Strategic investors such as Zoho and TDK Ventures have participated as anchor investors in funding rounds.
  • Global semiconductor companies are increasingly taking equity positions in Indian startups rather than limiting their presence to captive R&D centres.
  • Strategic investment can provide startups with access to fabs, tool credits, reference customers and design partnerships, in addition to funding.

Emerging Technology Areas

  • India's semiconductor startup ecosystem is expanding beyond its earlier focus on digital chips, RISC-V and edge SoCs.
  • Newer areas include:
    • Photonics
    • Power and compound semiconductors
    • Fab tooling and metrology
    • AI data-centre silicon
    • AI-driven semiconductor design workflows
    • Analogue AI inference

RBI Finalises Borrower Protection Rules for Loan Recovery from January 2027

  • The RBI’s final directions on loan recovery will come into force from January 1, 2027, introducing standardised rules for recovery-agent conduct, borrower contact, device restrictions and compensation for violations.

Key Borrower Safeguards

  • Recovery agents can contact borrowers only between 8 AM and 7 PM, unless the borrower consents to contact outside these hours.
  • A borrower must receive at least one day’s notice before the first physical recovery visit.
  • Borrowers must be informed if their recovery agency changes. Lenders must publish updated lists of their empanelled recovery agencies.
  • Physical-visit agents must carry identification, written authorisation, advance-visit notice and grievance-officer contact details.
  • Borrowers’ preferred contact time or mode should be respected, and unannounced visits are prohibited.
  • Repeated contacts on the same day after the borrower has already responded are prohibited.

Prohibited Recovery Practices

Recovery agents cannot use:

  • Abusive, threatening or intimidating language
  • Excessive calls or messages
  • Threatening or anonymous calls
  • Threats or acts of violence
  • Public humiliation of borrowers, relatives, friends or colleagues
  • Inappropriate messages
  • Online publication of borrowers’ videos, audio or personal information
  • False claims about the debt or consequences of non-payment

Device-Locking Rules

  • Lenders generally cannot remotely lock borrowers’ devices unless the device itself was financed.
  • For financed devices, locking is permitted only when:
    • The loan agreement allows it.
    • The borrower receives the required notices.
    • The device is subject to the prescribed overdue timeline.
    • The locking mechanism is manufacturer/operating-system certified, where available.
  • The rules provide a staged process before restrictions are imposed, with restrictions on a financed device possible only after the prescribed 90-day overdue timeline if dues remain unpaid.
  • Even when restrictions are permitted, calls, SMS and SOS functionality must remain active, with outgoing calls restricted only after the prescribed period.

Data & Privacy Protection

  • Lenders and their technology partners cannot access a borrower's contacts, messages, call logs, photographs or location history for loan recovery.
  • Safety and connectivity features of the device must also be protected.
  • The rules aim to prevent lenders from using device access or personal data as coercive recovery tools.

Compensation & Accountability

  • Lenders remain responsible for the conduct of their recovery agencies and agents.
  • Lenders must conduct due diligence and periodically verify recovery agents.
  • Recovery policies must provide for compensation for non-compliant recovery practices or agent misconduct.
  • Wrongful restrictions or delayed restoration can attract compensation of ₹250 per hour.
  • Once a borrower regularises the account, the lender must restore device functionality within one hour.
  • After full repayment, the lender must relinquish control of the device-locking mechanism and provide instructions for its removal where required.

Implementation Challenges

  • The rules apply a common, board-approved recovery policy across nine categories of regulated lenders, including banks, cooperative banks and NBFCs.
  • Compliance costs may rise because lenders will need systems for call-record preservation, agent verification and automated device unlocking.
  • Third-party subcontracting networks could make monitoring recovery agents more difficult.
  • There is also a risk that lenders could attempt to bypass contact-hour restrictions through unregulated digital channels, automated bots or informal skip-tracing.
  • Recovery calls and related records will need to be preserved for six months.

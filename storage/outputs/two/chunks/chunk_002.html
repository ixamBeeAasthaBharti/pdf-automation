RBI Finalises Borrower Protection Rules for Loan Recovery from January 2027
The RBI’s final directions on loan recovery will come into force from January
•
1, 2027 , introducing standardised rules for recovery-agent conduct, borrower
contact, device restrictions and compensation for violations.
Key Borrower Safeguards
•
Recovery agents can contact borrowers only between 8 AM and 7 PM , unless
the borrower consents to contact outside these hours.
A borrower must receive at least one day’s notice before the first physical
•
recovery visit .
•
Borrowers must be informed if their recovery agency changes.
Lenders must publish updated lists of their empanelled recovery agencies .
•
Physical-visit agents must carry identification, written authorisation, advance-visit
•
notice and grievance-officer contact details.
•
Borrowers’ preferred contact time or mode should be respected, and
unannounced visits are prohibited.
Repeated contacts on the same day after the borrower has already responded
•
are prohibited.
Prohibited Recovery Practices
Recovery agents cannot use:
•
o Abusive, threatening or intimidating language
o Excessive calls or messages
o Threatening or anonymous calls
o Threats or acts of violence
o Public humiliation of borrowers, relatives, friends or colleagues
o Inappropriate messages
o Online publication of borrowers’ videos, audio or personal information
o False claims about the debt or consequences of non-payment

Device-Locking Rules
Lenders generally cannot remotely lock borrowers’ devices unless the device
•
itself was financed .
•
For financed devices, locking is permitted only when:
o The loan agreement allows it.
o The borrower receives the required notices.
o The device is subject to the prescribed overdue timeline.
o The locking mechanism is manufacturer/operating-system certified, where
available.
The rules provide a staged process before restrictions are imposed, with
•
restrictions on a financed device possible only after the prescribed 90-day
overdue timeline if dues remain unpaid.
Even when restrictions are permitted, calls, SMS and SOS functionality must
•
remain active , with outgoing calls restricted only after the prescribed period.
Data & Privacy Protection
Lenders and their technology partners cannot access a borrower's contacts,
•
messages, call logs, photographs or location history for loan recovery.
•
Safety and connectivity features of the device must also be protected.
The rules aim to prevent lenders from using device access or personal data as
•
coercive recovery tools.
Compensation & Accountability
Lenders remain responsible for the conduct of their recovery agencies and
•
agents.
Lenders must conduct due diligence and periodically verify recovery agents.
•
•
Recovery policies must provide for compensation for non-compliant recovery
practices or agent misconduct .
Wrongful restrictions or delayed restoration can attract compensation of ₹250 per
•
hour .

Once a borrower regularises the account, the lender must restore device
•
functionality within one hour .
After full repayment, the lender must relinquish control of the device-locking
•
mechanism and provide instructions for its removal where required.
Implementation Challenges
The rules apply a common, board-approved recovery policy across nine
•
categories of regulated lenders , including banks, cooperative banks and
NBFCs.
•
Compliance costs may rise because lenders will need systems for call-record
preservation, agent verification and automated device unlocking.
Third-party subcontracting networks could make monitoring recovery agents
•
more difficult.
•
There is also a risk that lenders could attempt to bypass contact-hour restrictions
through unregulated digital channels, automated bots or informal skip-
tracing .
•
Recovery calls and related records will need to be preserved for six months .

Government to Announce First VC Fund Managers for ₹1 Trillion RDI Fund
The government is expected to announce the first cohort of nearly 20 venture
•
capital (VC) firms selected as second-level fund managers (SLFMs) for the ₹1
trillion Research, Development & Innovation (RDI) Fund in August 2026.
Key Facts
•
The RDI Fund has a total corpus of ₹1 trillion and aims to support research,
development and innovation, particularly technology-intensive companies and
startups.
IvyCap Ventures, Speciale Invest, Chiratae Ventures and Kalaari Capital are
•
among the firms shortlisted in the first cohort.
The first cohort is expected to comprise nearly 20 Alternative Investment
•
Funds (AIFs) , with a second cohort likely in October-November 2026 .
The government received nearly 200 applications for SLFMs, with around 80%
•
coming from VC firms .
Other eligible applicants include development finance institutions, non-
•
banking finance corporations and focused research organisations .
Government Investment Framework
The government can invest up to 50% of the amount in selected AIFs and
•
become a Limited Partner (LP) in the fund.
•
Applications generally involved fund sizes of around ₹2,000 -2,500 crore ,
meaning government participation could cover up to half of the fund size.
The Department of Science & Technology (DST) is the nodal ministry
•
responsible for the RDI Fund.
Selection Criteria
Fund managers are being selected through the Quality and Cost-Based
•
Selection (QCBS) method.
3

Quality carries 80% weight , focusing on the fund's ability to support startups
•
and scale RDI-intensive technologies.
Cost carries 20% weight , including management fees and related factors.
•
The evaluation also considers investment-management capability, experience in
•
supporting RDI-intensive technology companies and the ability to mobilise private
capital.
Budget & Implementation
The Union Budget 2026-27 allocated an additional ₹20,000 crore to the RDI
•
Fund.
The government has not fixed a specific amount for investment through AIFs.
•
BIRAC and the Technology Development Board (TDB) are nominated second-
•
level managers, with ₹2,000 crore earmarked for them in FY 2026-27.
•
The government may conduct additional rounds of applications for selecting
more second-level fund managers.
Glossary
RDI Fund: Research, Development & Innovation Fund created to support
•
innovation and RDI-intensive technologies.
AIF: Alternative Investment Fund, a privately pooled investment vehicle investing
•
in assets such as startups and other businesses.
SLFM: Second-Level Fund Manager, an investment partner selected to manage
•
investments under the RDI Fund.
QCBS: Quality and Cost-Based Selection, a selection method that evaluates
•
bidders based on both quality and cost.
Limited Partner (LP): An investor in a fund who contributes capital but generally
•

Weekly Swap Schedule Delays RBI Receipt of FCNR(B) Dollar Inflows
The RBI has assigned banks specific weekly slots to swap FCNR(B)-linked dollar
•
inflows, creating a time lag between deposit mobilisation by banks and the
transfer of dollars to the central bank.
FCNR(B) Swap Mechanism
•
Each participating bank can access the RBI's concessional dollar-rupee swap
window only once a week on its designated day.
Multiple banks may be assigned the same day.
•
•
Dollars mobilised during the previous week are generally swapped when the
bank's designated day arrives in the following week.
As a result, dollars raised late in a week may remain temporarily on banks' books
•
before being transferred to the RBI.
Impact on Forex Reserves
The weekly scheduling creates a lag between FCNR(B) mobilisation and the
•
RBI's receipt of dollars .
7
Finance Weekly – July 2026 Week 2
Therefore, the increase in banks' FCNR(B) deposits and the rise in the RBI's
•
foreign currency assets may not move together on a particular reporting date.
The timing of the swap depends on when deposits are mobilised relative to each
•
bank's assigned swap day.
RBI Swap Facility
The RBI announced the concessional dollar-rupee swap facility on June 5, 2026 ,
•
and operationalised it on June 8 .
The facility is available until September 30, 2026 .
•
•
It applies to fresh FCNR(B) deposits with original maturities of 3 to 5 years .
Banks can exchange the foreign currency raised through these deposits with the
•
RBI at the prevailing spot exchange rate , with the RBI providing the currency
back at maturity at a concessional cost.

•
data.

•

Sebi Proposes Wider FPI Participation in Non-Agricultural Commodity Derivatives
Sebi has proposed allowing Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) greater access
•
to exchange-traded non-agricultural commodity derivatives to improve liquidity,
market depth and price discovery while introducing safeguards against physical
delivery obligations.
Key Proposals
FPIs would be allowed to trade non-agricultural index derivatives irrespective
•
of whether the underlying commodity contracts are cash-settled or physically
settled.
•
Currently, FPIs can participate only in cash-settled non-agricultural
derivatives where the underlying contracts are also cash settled.
Sebi noted that index derivatives themselves are always cash settled , so their
•
underlying settlement mechanism does not create a delivery issue for FPIs.
•
FPIs would also be allowed to participate in physically settled/non-cash-
settled non-agricultural commodity derivatives traded on domestic
exchanges.
Commodities Covered
The proposal would cover derivatives linked to major commodities such as:
•
o Crude oil
18

o Natural gas
o Gold
o Silver
o Base metals
These commodities are closely linked to international benchmarks.
•
Benefits Expected
Broaden the participant base in India's commodity derivatives market.
•
Improve market liquidity and depth .
•
•
Strengthen price discovery .
Improve convergence between derivatives and physical commodity markets .
•
Increase integration of India's commodity derivatives market with global
•
commodity markets .
Safeguards for Physical Contracts
FPIs would have to square off or roll over their positions before the beginning
•
of the tender period, which starts three days before expiry (T-3) .
If an FPI fails to do so voluntarily, its open position would be automatically
•
transferred to the proprietary account of a designated Trading Member (TM) or
Trading-cum-Clearing Member (TCM).
•
The automatic transfer would occur after market hours on T-1 , at the
exchange's closing price or daily settlement price.
After the transfer, the FPI would have no further obligation or exposure related
•
to the position, including tender or physical delivery.
Two-Tier Risk Management
Sebi has proposed a layered/two-tier safeguard mechanism because relying
•
only on voluntary squaring-off could create risks.
•
FPIs would need either:
19
Finance Weekly – July 2026 Week 2
o A tripartite agreement with a Professional Clearing Member and Trading
Member, or
o A bipartite agreement with a Trading-cum-Clearing Member.
Exchanges would standardise the format and key terms of these agreements.
•
The designated TM/TCM would have up to two trading days to bring transferred
•
positions within its prescribed position limits.
The agreement may include a Proprietary Risk Absorption Charge payable by
•
the FPI for the risk and margin burden arising from an involuntary position
transfer.
Reason for Delivery Safeguard
FPIs are generally not permitted to take or make physical delivery in India
•
because they do not have a permanent establishment in the country.
•
Even if FPIs use brokers or trading members for delivery, they may be required
to obtain GST registration to buy or sell commodities in India.
The proposed mechanism therefore aims to prevent FPIs from reaching the
•
physical delivery stage.
Regulatory Timeline
Sebi's Commodity Derivatives Advisory Committee (CDAC) has supported
•
the proposal.
Sebi has invited public comments on the proposals and draft circular until
•
September 1, 2026 .

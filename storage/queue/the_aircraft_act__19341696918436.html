The Aircraft Act, 1934  
INTRODUCTION  
.
The Indian Airships Act of 1911 was the country's initial legislation in aviation sector. It  
was subsequently replaced by the Aircraft Act of 1934, which became the key legal  
framework for aviation.  
.
.
The Aircraft Amendment Bill of 2020 seeks to introduce necessary modifications to this  
Act.  
The Aircraft Act (“the Act”) aims to enhance regulations related to the:  
 manufacturing,  
 possession,  
 use,  
 operation,  
 sale,  
 import, and export of aircraft.  
.
The Act grants the government authority to establish rules and laws governing civil  
aviation in India, covering aspects like aircraft maintenance, airworthiness, registration,  
flight procedures, safety, and investigative procedures in case of incidents.  
In other words, it is an Act to make better provision for the control of the manufacture,  
possession, use, operation, sale, import and export of aircraft.  
The Act extends to the whole of India and applies to:  
 citizens of India wherever they may be;  
.
.
 to and to the persons on aircraft registered in India wherever they may be;  
 to and to the persons on aircraft registered outside India but for the time being in  
or over India; and  
 to an aircraft operated by a person who is not a citizen of India but has his principal  
place of business or permanent residence in India.  
SOME IMPORTANT DEFINTIONS SECTION 2  
.
.
.
Section 2 (1) "aircraft" means any machine which can derive support in the atmosphere  
from reactions of the air, other than reactions of the air against the earth's surface and  
includes balloons whether fixed or free, airships, kites, gliders and flying machines;  
Section 2 (2) "aerodrome" means any definite or limited ground or water area intended to  
be used, either wholly or in part, for the landing or departure of aircraft, and includes all  
buildings, sheds, vessels, piers and other structures thereon or appertaining thereto;  
Section 2 (2A) "aerodrome reference point", in relation to any aerodrome, means a  
designated point established in the horizontal plane at or near the geometric center of that  
part of the aerodrome reserved for the departure or landing of aircraft;  
.
.
Section 2 (3) "import" means bringing into India; and  
Section 2 (4) "export" means taking out of India.

.
.
The Central Government can exempt specific aircraft or individuals from certain  
provisions of this Act through a notification in the Official Gazette.

CONVENTION OF 1944 SECTION 4  
.
.

POWER TO DETAIN AIRCRAFT SECTION 8  
.
Any authority authorised in this behalf by the Central Government may detain any  
aircraft, if in the opinion of such authority:  
 having regard to the nature of an intended flight, the flight of such aircraft would  
involve danger to persons in the aircraft or to any other persons or property; or  
 such detention is necessary to secure compliance with any of the provisions of this  
Act or the rules applicable to such aircraft; or such detention is necessary to  
prevent a contravention of any rule made under clause (h) or clause (i) of section  
5 (2) or to implement any order made by any Court.

WRECK AND SALVAGE SECTION 9  
.
The provisions of Part XIII of the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958, regarding Wreck and  
Salvage, shall also be applicable to aircraft over the sea or tidal water, similar to how  
they apply to ships.  
.
.
Aircraft owners have the right to receive reasonable compensation for salvage services  
provided by their aircraft, akin to ship owners.  
The Central Government has the authority to make necessary modifications to these  
provisions when applied to aircraft.

COMPOSITION OF OFFENCES SECTION 12A  
.
.
Offences punishable under sections 10, 11, 11A, 11B, and section 12 may be  
compounded either before or after initiating any prosecution.  
The compounding can be done by the Director General of Civil Aviation, Director  
General of Bureau of Civil Aviation Security, or Director General of Aircraft Accidents  
Investigation Bureau, as the case may be.  
.
Offences can be compounded under the following conditions:  
 Offences can be compounded by designated officers for the first occurrence within  
five years from the date of a similar prior offence that was either compounded or  
resulted in a conviction.  
 Designated officers' power to compound offences is subject to the Central  
Government's direction, control, and supervision.  
 Rules for applying for the compounding of an offence will be determined by the  
Central Government through notifications in the Official Gazette.  
 If an offence is compounded before the initiation of any prosecution, no further  
prosecution can be launched against the offender for that particular offence.  
 If an offence is compounded after the initiation of a prosecution, the designated  
officer must inform the court handling the prosecution in writing. Upon receiving

The Aircraft Act, 1934  
notice of the compounded offence, the person being prosecuted will be  
discharged.  
 Compounding an offence under this section will have the same effect as an  
acquittal of the accused.  
COGNIZANCE OF OFFENCES SECTION 12B  
.
.
.
No court shall take cognizance of any offence punishable under this Act, save on a  
complaint made by or with the previous sanction in writing by the Director General  
of Civil Aviation or Director General of Bureau of Civil Aviation Security or Director General  
of Aircraft Accidents Investigation Bureau, as the case may be.  
The complaint shall be made within a period of one year from the date on which the  
offence came to the knowledge of the Director General of Civil Aviation or Director General  
of Bureau of Civil Aviation Security or Director General of Aircraft Accidents Investigation  
Bureau, as the case may be.  
No court inferior to that of a Metropolitan Magistrate or a Magistrate of the first class  
shall try the offences under this Act.  
POWER OF COURT TO ORDER FORFEITURE SECTION 13  
.
If a person is convicted of an offence under section 10(1) or under any rule created under  
section 5(2)(i), the convicting court may order the forfeiture of the aircraft, article, or  
substance involved in the offence to the Government.

Securities and Exchange Board of India

SEBI was established in 1988 but received statutory powers on April 12, 1992, in

  • accordance with the provisions of the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992. SEBI is the market regulator for Capital Markets and Commodity markets. The Forward
  • Market Commission (former regulator for commodity markets) was merged with SEBI on Sep 28, 2015. SEBI discharges its role as regulator of securities market under the following Acts:
  • SEBI Act, 1992 ◦ Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 ◦ Depositaries Act, 1996 ◦ Companies Act, 2013 ◦

Other Functions of SEBI

The functions of SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) encompass a wide range aimed at ensuring the smooth operation and integrity of the securities market in India.

  • 1. Regulatory Functions Registration and Regulation: SEBI registers and regulates the working of
    • stockbrokers, sub-brokers, and other intermediaries in the securities market. It ensures that these entities comply with the rules and regulations. Regulation of Stock Exchanges: SEBI regulates stock exchanges to ensure that they
    • operate in a fair, transparent, and efficient manner. Prohibition of Insider Trading: SEBI enforces regulations that prevent insider trading,
    • ensuring that no participant gains an unfair advantage through non-public information. Regulation of Takeovers and Mergers: SEBI regulates mergers, acquisitions, and
    • www.ixamBee.com substantial takeovers to ensure that they are conducted in a transparent and fair
      • manner.
  • 2. Protective Functions Prohibition of Fraudulent Practices: SEBI has the authority to prohibit fraudulent and
    • unfair trade practices related to the securities market. It ensures that market participants adhere to ethical standards. Investor Protection: SEBI plays a crucial role in protecting investors from unethical
    • market practices. It ensures that investors receive correct and timely information to make informed decisions. Education and Awareness: SEBI conducts investor education programs to increase
    • awareness and knowledge about the securities market.
  • 3. Developmental Functions Promotion of Fair Practices: SEBI promotes fair practices by intermediaries, ensuring
    • that they operate in the best interest of investors. Market Development: SEBI promotes the development of the securities market by
    • introducing new products, services, and financial instruments that cater to the needs of investors and issuers. Research and Development: SEBI conducts research to identify and address issues in
    • the securities market, ensuring continuous improvement and innovation.
  • 4. Enforcement Functions Conducting Investigations: SEBI has the power to conduct investigations and audits of
    • market participants to detect and prevent any irregularities. Adjudication: SEBI can adjudicate and pass orders in cases of violations of its
      • regulations. This includes imposing penalties on defaulters.

Dispute Resolution: SEBI has mechanisms in place for the resolution of disputes

  • between market participants, including investors and intermediaries.

Role of SEBI as Market Regulator

Protecting Investors: SEBI ensures that the interests of investors are safeguarded by regulating the securities

  • market, preventing fraudulent practices, and ensuring that markets operate
    • transparently.

Example: SEBI took action against Sahara India Real Estate Corporation Limited and

  • Sahara Housing Investment Corporation Limited for raising funds from the public without proper disclosures, which was a violation of securities laws. SEBI ordered the companies to refund the money collected to the investors with interest, thus protecting the investors from potential losses due to misleading investment schemes.

www.ixamBee.comEnsuring Fair, Efficient, and Transparent Markets: SEBI is responsible for maintaining the integrity of the securities market. It ensures that

  • the markets function efficiently and that all market participants have access to accurate and timely information, thus fostering a level playing field. Example: SEBI introduced the "Know Your Client (KYC)" norms to ensure that all
  • market participants are properly identified, which prevents fraudulent activities and money laundering. This initiative helps maintain a fair and transparent market by ensuring that all investors' identities are verified before they participate in the securities
    • market.

Reducing Systemic Risk: SEBI works to minimise the risks that could lead to a systemic failure of the financial

    • markets. It ensures that market intermediaries and participants adhere to regulations

that prevent practices which could destabilise the market. Example: During the 2008 global financial crisis, SEBI introduced regulations for

  • credit rating agencies to enhance transparency and accountability. By ensuring that credit rating agencies operate with greater oversight and integrity, SEBI helped reduce the risk of systemic failures in the financial market that could arise from inaccurate or biased credit ratings.

Promoting Investor Confidence: By maintaining a fair and transparent market, SEBI fosters investor confidence. It also

  • engages in investor education to make them aware of their rights and the functioning of the securities market. Example: SEBI launched the SCORES 2.0 (SEBI Complaints Redress System)
  • platform, which allows investors to lodge complaints online against listed companies and market intermediaries. This system ensures that investors' grievances are addressed in a timely manner, thus boosting their confidence in the securities market.

Regulatory Jurisdiction: SEBI’s regulatory jurisdiction extends over corporates in the issuance of capital and

  • transfer of securities. This includes all intermediaries and persons associated with the securities market. Example: SEBI's regulatory jurisdiction was demonstrated when it imposed restrictions
  • on ICICI Bank’s initial public offering (IPO) due to concerns about the valuation of its
    • shares. SEBI ensured that the shares were priced fairly and that all necessary

disclosures were made, protecting both the market's integrity and the interests of potential investors.

Conducting Inquiries, Audits, and Inspections: SEBI has the authority to conduct investigations, audits, and inspections of market

  • participants, including stock exchanges, mutual funds, and other intermediaries. It can also adjudicate offenses under the SEBI Act. Example: SEBI conducted an audit of NSE (National Stock Exchange) to investigate
  • www.ixamBee.com allegations of preferential access to certain brokers, which became known as the "co- location" case. SEBI's investigation revealed that some brokers received unfair advantages, leading to enforcement actions, including penalties and stricter regulations for market exchanges.

Registration and Regulation of Market Intermediaries: SEBI has the power to register and regulate all market intermediaries, such as

  • stockbrokers, sub-brokers, and others. It can impose penalties for violations of the Act, rules, and regulations. Example: SEBI mandates that mutual fund managers must be registered and follow
  • stringent guidelines regarding the management of funds. When SEBI found violations in the management practices of mutual funds by Franklin Templeton India, it ordered the closure of certain debt schemes and ensured that investors were refunded their
    • investments.

Taking Enforcement Actions: If SEBI has reasonable grounds to believe that any company or individual has engaged

  • in insider trading, fraudulent, or unfair trade practices, it can take various enforcement
    • actions.

Example: SEBI banned Ketan Parekh, a stockbroker involved in the 2001 stock

  • market manipulation scam, from trading in the securities market. SEBI found that Parekh was involved in rigging stock prices to his advantage, leading to significant market
    • disruption. SEBI’s enforcement action prevented further market manipulation and

protected investor interests.

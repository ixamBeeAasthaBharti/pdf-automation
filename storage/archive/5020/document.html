Introduction

  • There are two types of receipt
    • Capital Receipts: Receipts which either create liability or reduce assets
    • Revenue Receipts: Receipts that neither generate liability nor reduce assets
  • Capital receipts are loans taken by the government from the public, borrowings from foreign countries, institutes, and RBI
  • Recovery of loans given by the Centre to states and others is also Figure 1: Components of Government Receipts included in capital receipt Source: web search
  • The capital receipt has a nature of non-recurrence.
  • All capital receipts are tax-free, unless there is a proviso to tax it.
  • Revenue Receipts cannot be reclaimed back by the citizens from the government (non- redeemable).
  • Revenue Receipts are classified into two main categories
    • Tax revenue receipts: Come from direct taxes or indirect taxes. (Discussed in detail in separate chapter)
    • Non-Tax revenue Receipts: Those receipts which are not generated by taxing the public, example
      • Money, that Government earns as “Dividends and profits” from its profit making public enterprises (PSUs).
      • Interest earned by Government on the money lent to external or internal borrowers.
      • Money that government receives out of its fiscal services such as stamp printing, currency printing, medal printing etc.
      • Money earned by the Government earned by providing “General Services” such as power distribution, irrigation, banking services, insurance, and community services etc.
      • Fees, fines, penalties etc. are accrued by the government
      • Grants received from the external sources.
  • Capital receipts can be both non-debt and debt receipts
    • Non-debt receipts are those which do not incur any future repayment burden for the government.
      • Almost 75 per cent of the total budget receipts are non-debt receipts.
  • Example: Recovery of loans and advances, disinvestment, issue of bonus shares, etc.
  • Debt Receipts have to be repaid by the government
    • Around 25 per cent of government expenditure is financed through borrowing
    • Example: Market loans, issuance of special securities to public-sector banks, issue of securities, short-term borrowings, treasury bills, securities against small savings, state provident funds, relief bonds, saving bonds, gold bonds, external debt, etc.

Tax: Introduction

  • Tax is a compulsory payment levied on the persons or companies to meet the expenditure incurred on conferring common benefits upon the people of the country
  • Two aspects of taxes are
    • A tax is a compulsory payment and no one can refuse to pay it
    • Proceeds from tax are used for common benefits
  • Fee is compulsory payment made by a person who receives in return a particular benefit or service from the government
  • Classification of taxes
    • Direct and Indirect Taxes
    • Specific and Ad-Valorem Taxes
    • Progressive, Proportional, Regressive Taxes and Digressive

Principles or canons of good tax system

  • Four canons (Adam Smith)
    • Equality
    • Certainty
    • Convenience
    • Economy
  • Other Principles or characteristics of a good taxation system
    • Productivity or Fiscal Adequacy
    • Elasticity of Taxation
    • Diversity
    • Taxation as an instrument of Economic Growth
    • Taxation as an instrument for improving income distribution
    • Taxation for ensuring Economic Stability
  • A good tax system should possess the following characteristics
    • Revenue Adequacy: A good tax system should generate adequate revenue for the government
    • Equity: tax burden should be distributed in equitable manner
    • Efficiency: Tax system should not interfere with the efficient allocation of resources
    • Administrative Simplicity: The tax structure should be simple to administer
  • Transparency: The levying of taxes and spending of tax proceeds should be done in a transparent manner.
  • Principle of Equity in Taxation
    • Benefit Receive theory: As per it, the individuals should contribute to government revenue according to the benefits they receive from public services
    • Ability to pay theory: It states that individuals should contribute towards government revenue according to their ability to pay.

Carbonyl group

  • The organic compounds which contain carbon-oxygen double bond (>C=O) are called as carbonyl compounds.
  • This is one of the most important functional group in organic chemistry. The carbonyl carbon atom is –sp2hybridised and forms three sigma (σ) bonds (two with
  • adjacent hydrogen groups and one with oxygen) and one pi (Π) bond (with oxygen atom).

What are Aldehydes?

  • In aldehydes, the carbonyl is bonded to a carbon and hydrogen.
  • In other words, any of a class of organic compounds in which a carbon atom shares a double bond with an oxygen atom, a single bond with a hydrogen atom, and a single bond with another atom or group of atoms (designated R in general chemical formulas and structure diagrams).
  • The general formula for this group is-CnH2nO.
  • They are represented as-

O || R-C-H

  • For example- For n=2 CnH2nO= C (n)=2; H (2n)=2x2=4 Hence, the structure of the compound will be C2H4O.

Similarly, for n=3; CnH2nO= C (n)=3; H (2n)=2 x 3=6 The compound will be C3H6O.

Nomenclature

  • Aldehydes are often called by their common names instead of their IUPAC name.
  • The common names of most aldehydes are derived from the common names of the corresponding carboxylic acids by replacing the ending –ic of acid with aldehyde.
  • The location of the substituent in the carbon chain is indicated by Greek letters α, β, γ, δ, etc.

IUPAC Nomenclature

  • The IUPAC names of open chain aldehydes are derived from the names of the corresponding alkanes by replacing the –e with –al.
  • Firstly, the longest carbon chain is numbered starting from the carbon of the aldehyde group.
  • The substituents are prefixed in alphabetical order along with numerals indicating their positions in the carbon chain.

Physical properties

  • Lower members of aldehydes (upto C10) are colourless, volatile liquids except formaldehyde, which is gas at room temperature.
  • Higher members of aldehydes are solids with fruity odour.
  • Lower aldehydes have sharp pungent odour.

Boiling point and solubility

  • The boiling of aldehydes are higher than hydrocarbons and ethers of comparable molecular masses due to weak dipole-dipole interactions.
  • Their boiling points are lower than alcohols of similar molecular masses due to absence of intermolecular hydrogen-bonding.
  • Lower members of aldehydes are soluble in water.
  • They are fairly soluble in organic solvents like benzene, ether, methanol, chloroform, etc.
  • Their solubility decreases rapidly on increasing the length of alkyl chain.

Uses of aldehydes in everyday life

  • Aldehydes are used as solvents in chemical industry.
  • These are also used as starting materials and reagents for the synthesis of other organic compounds.
  • Formalin (40% solution of formaldehyde) is used to preserve biological specimens.
  • Formaldehyde is also used for the synthesis of Bakelite (phenol-formaldehyde resin), urea-formaldehyde glues and other polymeric products.

Study notes (Aldehydes)

  • Acetaldehyde (CH3CHO) is used as starting material for the manufacture of acetic acid, ethyl acetate, vinyl acetate, polymers and drugs.
  • Benzaldehyde is used in perfumery and in dye industries.
  • Butyraldehyde, vanillin (from vanilla beans), etc. are used as flavoring agents.

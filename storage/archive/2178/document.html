Note:

  • Currently, Narrow Money (M1) and Broad Money (M3) are relevant indicators of money supply in India
  • The major distinction between the M1 (narrow money) and M3 (broad money) is with respect to the deposits with the banks. M3 includes the time deposits also in addition to the demand deposits with the banks.
  • The distinction between M1/M3 and M2/M4 respectively is related to the Post Office Savings. Now, M2 and M4 are irrelevant as Post office Savings is mostly negligible after the rise and prominence of scheduled banks over the years. As such, the data related to Post office savings is also not frequently updated by RBI.

Liquidity Relation among the Monetary Aggregates

M1>M2>M3>M4

  • M1 is the most liquid form of money
  • M4 is the least liquid form of money

Money Multiplier

  • Money Multiplier is the ratio of the Narrow Money (M1) or the Broad Money (M3) to Reserve Money (M0)
  • Money multiplier (m) = 1/Reserve ratio requirement
  • Money Supply = m * M0
  • The amount money that banks generate with each unit of reserve money is through the money multiplier.
  • As such, the supply of money is product of Money Multiplier (m) and the amount of high powered money or the reserve money.

For example let‟s say total deposit in banking system is Rs.100 crore and reserve ratio requirement is 5%.The banks can lend 95% of deposit i.e. Rs.95 crore. This Rs.95 crore that banks will lend to its customers will ultimately be deposited in another bank which can further lend 95% of that i.e. Rs.90.25 crore and cycle continues.

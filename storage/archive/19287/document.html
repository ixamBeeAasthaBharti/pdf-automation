Statements mandated under the FRBM Act

The FRBM Act was launched primarily for the management of the governments' behavior, hence it provided certain documents to be tabled in the parliament annually with regards to the country's fiscal policy. This included the following along with the Annual Financial Statement and demands for grants:

  • The Medium Term Fiscal Policy Statement - This statement was to present a three- year rolling target for the fiscal indicators with any assumptions, if applicable. This statement also included an assessment of sustainability with regards to revenue deficit and the use of capital receipts of the Government for generating productive assets.
  • The Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement - This is a tactical report enumerating strategies and policies for the upcoming Financial Year including strategic fiscal priorities, taxation policies, key fiscal measures and an evaluation of how the proposed policies of the Central Government conform to the 'Fiscal Management Principles' of this
    • act.

FRBM Review Committee of 2016

  • Finance Minister Arun Jaitley announced the FRBM Review Committee to be set up in the budget speech of February 2016–17 to evaluate the FRBM Act, 2003 in order to assess its functionality since its inception.
  • This committee was headed by Mr N.K. Singh, submitted its report in January 2017. The Committee proposed a draft Debt Management and Fiscal Responsibility Bill, 2017 to replace the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2003 (FRBM Act)
  • Key recommendations of the committee were:
    • Debt-to-GDP ratio – The committee suggested to use “debt” as the primary target for fiscal policy. A debt-GDP ratio of 60% should be targeted with a 40% limit for the center and 20% limit for the states. The targeted ratio should be achievable by 2023. 5Revision in Glide path – 2019

  • The Economic Survey 2018-19 stated that the General Government (Centre plus States) has been on the path of fiscal consolidation and fiscal discipline.
  • However, it could not strictly adhere to the FRBM Act glide path due to structural disruptions such as GST
  • As such, the fiscal glide path was revised as follows:
    • Achieving fiscal deficit of 3% of GDP by FY 2020-21 (as against by FY2019-20 i.e. extended by 1 year)
    • Central Government debt to 40% of GDP by 2024-25 (as against by FY2022-23, i.e. extended by 2 years)

Escape Clause under the FRBM Act

  • The NK Singh committee added an escape clause in its recommendations, allowing the government slippages up to 50 basis points or 0.5 per cent of the GDP in case of extraordinary circumstances.
  • This relaxation was provided under Section 4(2) of the Act, which allows the Centre to exceed the annual fiscal deficit target citing certain grounds.
  • The grounds include
    • National security, war
    • National calamity
    • Collapse of agriculture severely affecting farm output and incomes
    • Structural reforms with unanticipated fiscal implications
    • Decline in real output growth of a quarter by at least 3 percentage points below the average of the previous four quarters.
  • During the global financial crisis in 2008-09:
    • The Centre resorted to a focused fiscal stimulus: tax relief to boost demand and increased expenditure on public projects to create employment and public assets, to counter the fallout of the global slowdown.
    • This led to the fiscal deficit climbing to 6.2%, from a budgeted goal of 2.7%.
    • Simultaneously, the deficit goals for the States too were relaxed to 3.5% of Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) for 2008-09 and 4% of GSDP for fiscal 2009-10.
  • Use of Escape clause in view of COVID-19:
    • The Fiscal deficit for 2019-20 has been reported as 4.6% of the GDP, as per the latest data provided by the Controller General of Accounts (CGA).
    • The lockdown could cause severe contraction in economic output and the COVID-19 pandemic could be considered as a national calamity.
    • Also, the government has already made the use of escape clause this year.
    • Union Government has provided the states with the additional 0.5 per cent leeway (in addition to 3% of GSDP) provided to them under the Act to deal with extraordinary situations.
  • In view of the COVID-19 situation, and the unprecedented nature of the pandemic and its devastating impact on the global economy, another significant deviation from the FRBM norms is very likely in FY20-21 and next fiscal years
  • The fiscal glide path has been revised to setting of a target of 3.1% for March 2023

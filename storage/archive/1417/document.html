  • 49. Inflation theory which combines both the Pull Back and Push Back to explain the inflation

A. Market Power Theory B. Mark up theory C. Bottle Neck theory D. Monetary Theory E. None of the given options Ans: D Basically, there are 3 main theories of inflation,

  • I. Market power Theory
  • II. Conventional demand-pull inflation
  • III. Structural theory of inflation

Structural theory:

According to structural theory of inflation, market power is one of the factors that cause inflation, but it is not the only factor. The supporters of structural theories believed that inflation arises due to structural maladjustments in the county or some of the institutional features of the business environment. Structural theory considers both Pull Back and Push Back to explain the inflation. Structural theory further divided into two more theories

  • I. Mark up theory
  • II. Bottleneck theory

Mark-up Theory:

Mark-up theory of inflation was proposed by Prof Gardner Ackley. According to him, inflation cannot occur alone by demand and cost factors, but it is the cumulative effect of demand-pull and cost-push activities. Demand-pull inflation refers to the inflation that occurs due to excess of aggregate demand, which further results in the increases in the price level. The increase in prices levels stimulates production but increases demand for factors of production. Consequently, the cost and price both

  • increases.

In some cases, wages also increase without rise in the excess demand of products. This results in a fall in supply at the increased level of prices as to compensate the increase in wages with the prices of

  • products. The shortage of products in the market would result in a further increase in prices.

Therefore, Prof. Gardner has provided a model of mark-up inflation in which both the factors, demand cost, are determined. Increase in demand results in the increase of prices of products as the customers spend more on products. On the other the goods are sold to businesses instead of customers, then the cost of production

  • increases. As a result, the prices of products also increase. Similarly, a rise in wages results in an

increase in the cost of production, which would further increase the prices of products. So according to Prof Gardner, inflation occurs due to excess of demand or increases in wage rates; therefore, both monetary and fiscal policies should be used to control inflation. Though, these two policies are not adequate to control inflation. Bottle-neck inflation Theory: Bottle-neck inflation was introduced by Prof Otto Eckstein. According to him, the direct relationship between wages and prices of products is the main cause of inflation. In other words, inflation takes place when there is a simultaneous increase in wages and prices of products. However, he believed that wage push or market-power theories alone are not able to provide a clear explanation of inflation. After analysis of the inflationary situation, Prof Eckstein says that the inflation occurs due to the boom in capital goods and wage-price spiral. In addition, he also advocated that during inflation prices in every industry is higher, but few industries show a very high price hike than the rest of the industries.

GDP is the final value of the goods and services produced within the geographic boundaries of a country during a specified period of time, normally a year. GDP growth rate is an important indicator of the economic performance of a country. GDP can be measured by three methods, namely,

  • 1. Output Method: This measures the monetary or market value of all the goods and services produced

within the borders of the country. In order to avoid a distorted measure of GDP due to price level changes, GDP at constant prices of real GDP is computed. GDP (as per output method) = Real GDP (GDP at constant prices) – Taxes + Subsidies.

  • 2. Expenditure Method: This measures the total expenditure incurred by all entities on goods and

services within the domestic boundaries of a country. GDP (as per expenditure method) = C + I + G + (X-M) C: Consumption expenditure, I: Investment expenditure, G: Government spending and (X-IM): Exports minus imports, that is net exports. Option A) Government outlay means government spending it includes all government consumption, investment, and transfer payments. It forms part (G) of GDP calculation. Option B) Private Inventory, The annual change in inventory is included in GDP. Inventory investment is more of an indicator. For example:

  • If production is greater than sales, inventories increases
  • If production is less than sales, inventories decrease

Option D) Exports forms part E of GDP calculations.

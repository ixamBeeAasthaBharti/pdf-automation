Carbonyl group

The organic compounds which contain carbon-oxygen double bond (  C=O) are called as
carbonyl compounds.


This is one of the most important functional group in organic chemistry.
The carbonyl carbon atom is – sp 2 hybridised and forms three sigma (  ) bonds (two with
adjacent hydrogen groups and one with oxygen) and one pi (  ) bond (with oxygen
atom).
What are Aldehydes?


In aldehydes, the carbonyl is bonded to a carbon and hydrogen.
In other words, any of a class of organic compounds in which a carbon atom shares a
double bond with an oxygen atom, a single bond with a hydrogen atom, and a single
bond with another atom or group of atoms (designated R in general chemical formulas
and structure diagrams).



Physical properties

Lower members of aldehydes (upto C 10 ) are colourless, volatile liquids except
formaldehyde, which is gas at room temperature.


Higher members of aldehydes are solids with fruity odour.
Lower aldehydes have sharp pungent odour.
Boiling point and solubility


The boiling of aldehydes are higher than hydrocarbons and ethers of comparable
molecular masses due to weak dipole-dipole interactions.
Their boiling points are lower than alcohols of similar molecular masses due to absence
of intermolecular hydrogen-bonding.



Lower members of aldehydes are soluble in water.
They are fairly soluble in organic solvents like benzene, ether, methanol, chloroform, etc.
Their solubility decreases rapidly on increasing the length of alkyl chain.
Uses of aldehydes in everyday life


Aldehydes are used as solvents in chemical industry.
These are also used as starting materials and reagents for the synthesis of other organic
compounds.


Formalin (40% solution of formaldehyde) is used to preserve biological specimens.
Formaldehyde is also used for the synthesis of Bakelite (phenol-formaldehyde resin),
urea-formaldehyde glues and other polymeric products.
